How to Write a Recruiting Follow-Up Email Clients Actually Answer

By Jay Tyler · November 24, 2026

Most follow-ups fail for one reason: they ask for nothing specific. Give the reader an easy next step and replies get easier.

You sent profiles, or a proposal, and heard nothing. The instinct is to write "just checking in." That line gives the client no reason to respond. Try these four formats instead.

1. The one-question follow-up

Ask a single question that takes ten seconds to answer: "Of the three profiles I sent, which one should I prioritize?" A narrow question beats an open invitation.

2. The new-information follow-up

Bring something new: a candidate who just became available, a pay-range data point, or a hiring trend in their market. You are adding value, not nagging.

3. The deadline follow-up

If a strong candidate is interviewing elsewhere, say so plainly: "She has another conversation Thursday. Can we get feedback by Wednesday?" Only use real deadlines.

4. The graceful close-the-loop follow-up

After several tries, send a short note: "I'll pause outreach on this role. If priorities change, I'm easy to reach." This respectful close often gets the reply the earlier emails did not.

Keep every follow-up short

Aim for under 80 words. Lead with the specific ask, use one clear next step, and skip apologies. Send at a sensible cadence, such as day 2, day 5, and day 10, and log each touch so nothing slips.

Where AI helps

Use AI to draft a few variations and tighten the wording, then edit so it sounds like you. Check every fact and name before sending. Never paste confidential client details into a tool you do not trust.

The bottom line

Be specific, bring something new, respect their time, and know when to close the loop. Good follow-up is a habit, not a pushy sequence.
