The 20-Minute Client Intake Call That Gets You Better Job Orders

By Jay Tyler · November 13, 2026

Most weak job orders start with a rushed first call. Twenty focused minutes up front can save you weeks of chasing a role nobody is serious about.

You landed the meeting. Now what? Many recruiters spend that call talking about their own firm and leave with a one-line req. Here is a simple agenda that does the opposite.

Minutes 0-3: Set the frame

Say what you'll cover and how long it takes: "I'd like to learn how you hire, then talk about the roles you have open, then agree on next steps. Does that work?" Clients relax when someone else runs a clear process.

Minutes 3-8: Learn how they hire

Ask before you pitch:

  • How do you fill roles today, and what has worked or not?
  • Who makes the final decision, and who else weighs in?
  • How fast do you usually move from interview to offer?
  • What happened with the last person in this job?

The last question often tells you more than the job description does.

Minutes 8-14: Get the real job order

Go past the title. Ask about the must-haves versus the nice-to-haves, the pay range, the schedule, the team, and what a great first 90 days looks like. Ask why the role is open now and what happens if it stays open another month. Urgency you can hear is urgency you can plan around.

Minutes 14-18: Agree on how you'll work together

Be direct about the process: how fast you'll send profiles, how many, and how quickly you need feedback. If the client wants speed, they need to give fast feedback. Say that plainly and kindly.

Minutes 18-20: Confirm next steps in writing

Recap the role, the timeline and what each side will do. Send the recap by email the same day. It becomes your working job order and protects both sides from drift.

Where AI helps

Use AI before the call to research the company and draft questions, and after it to turn your notes into a clean recap. Always check the facts yourself and keep the client's words in your own voice.

The bottom line

A good intake call is a conversation, not an interview of you. Learn how they hire, get specifics, set expectations, and send the recap. You'll work fewer bad reqs and fill more good ones.
